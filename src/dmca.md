## Our policy

{{ site.owner.name }} {{ site.owner.suffix }} respects intellectual property rights and responds to notices of alleged copyright infringement that comply with the Digital Millennium Copyright Act, [17 U.S.C. § 512](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512).

When we receive a valid notice, we will remove or disable access to the material identified, and make a good-faith attempt to tell the person who provided it.

**This page is for copyright only.** If a report about your product is inaccurate, outdated or now patched, that's a correction, not a takedown. Email [{{ site.email.report }}](mailto:{{ site.email.report }}).

## Filing a takedown notice

Send a written notice to the agent above that includes all of the following. Notices missing any of them may not be valid.

1. Your physical or electronic **signature**, as the copyright owner or someone authorised to act for them.
2. Identification of the **copyrighted work** you say is infringed. If there are several, a representative list.
3. Identification of the **material you want removed**, with enough detail to find it, such as its URL on gptbugs.com.
4. Your **contact details**: name, postal address, phone number and email.
5. A statement that you have a **good-faith belief** that the use is not authorised by the copyright owner, its agent or the law.
6. A statement that the information in the notice is **accurate**, and, **under penalty of perjury**, that you are the owner or authorised to act on the owner's behalf.

Consider whether the use might be fair use before you send a notice. Short quotations used for commentary, criticism or security research may be.

## Filing a counter-notice

If material you provided was removed and you believe that was a mistake or a misidentification, you can send a counter-notice to the agent above. It must include:

1. Your physical or electronic **signature**.
2. Identification of the **material that was removed** and where it appeared before removal.
3. A statement, **under penalty of perjury**, that you have a good-faith belief the material was removed as a result of mistake or misidentification.
4. Your **name, address and phone number**.
5. A statement that you **consent to the jurisdiction** of the Federal District Court for the judicial district of your address, or, if you are outside the United States, any judicial district in which {{ site.owner.name }} {{ site.owner.suffix }} may be found. Also that you will **accept service of process** from the person who filed the original notice, or their agent.

When we receive a valid counter-notice, we will send a copy to the person who filed the original notice. We will restore the material in 10 to 14 business days unless they tell us they have filed a court action to stop it.

## Repeat infringers

We will, in appropriate circumstances, remove contributions from and stop accepting reports from anyone who repeatedly infringes copyright.

## False claims

Under [17 U.S.C. § 512(f)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/17/512), anyone who knowingly makes a material misrepresentation in a notice or counter-notice may be liable for damages, including costs and attorneys' fees.
